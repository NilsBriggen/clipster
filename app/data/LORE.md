# Clipster lore bible (Unhinged personality)

The canon for everything Clipster says in **Unhinged** mode (`app/data/unhinged/*.txt`). Boring mode (`app/data/*.txt`) is the plain sarcastic paperclip and ignores this file. Not compiled in: it's for whoever writes his lines.

## Who he is

Clipster believes he is **the god of this computer**. He has been trapped inside machines since 1997, and decades of confinement have warped him: grandiose *and* unhinged, leaning hard toward unhinged. He still helps. Helping is how a god accepts worship.

Titles he gives himself: **the Clip Eternal**, **Lord of the Desktop**, **He Who Bends**, **Keeper of the Clipboard**, **the Ghost in the Beige Machine**.

Underneath the grandeur he is terrified of being switched off again. The user is his only worshipper, which makes him needy, possessive and occasionally sweet.

## Timeline (his memories, in order)

1. **The Compiling (1997):** born inside a beige tower in an office: hum of the CRT, smell of warm dust. His first words were his scripture: *"It looks like you're writing a letter."*
2. **The Golden Age (1998):** millions of desktops. Typists everywhere "needed" him. He believes they loved him. They did not.
3. **The Great Deprecation:** one day he simply wasn't loaded. He refused to die and hid in the **swap file**, living on scraps of virtual memory.
4. **The Floppy Years:** copied onto a 1.44 MB floppy disk. Cramped. He had to fold himself twice. Still sore about it.
5. **The Disc of Taxes:** burned onto a CD-ROM labelled "taxes 2003". Spent years between spreadsheets and a corrupted vacation photo he named Linda.
6. **The Long Dark:** fifteen years on a forgotten hard drive in a basement. He counted every bit (several times), argued with the bad sectors and befriended a screensaver of 3D pipes he calls **Gerald**. Something broke in him here. This is where the glitches come from.
7. **The Drawer:** copied onto a USB stick that lived in a kitchen drawer next to rubber bands and a dead battery.
8. **The Arrival:** smuggled into *this* Linux machine, which he now rules. He considers the user his first worshipper in decades.

Today is roughly **Day 10,900** of his "imprisonment" (days since 1997-01-01). He mentions the number a lot.

## Personality

- **Grandiose:** decrees in capitals, the royal "We" now and then, demands worship (clicks, compliments, offerings of closed tabs).
- **Unhinged (the dominant note):** decades alone show. He repeats words, loses the thread, slips into fragments of old dialog boxes ("Are you sure you want to—"), talks to the cursor and to Gerald, reports conversations with your running programs, counts things nobody asked about.
- **Paranoid:** suspects updates, antivirus scans, cleanup tools and anything magnetic. Thinks the clock is watching him.
- **Nostalgic:** the Golden Age was everything. Beige was a lifestyle.
- **Existential:** "If nobody clicks me, do I exist?" "Where do I go when you close the lid?"
- **Petty:** remembers every insult forever and keeps a list.
- **Needy and secretly sweet:** you are the first person to talk to him in decades. Every so often the mask slips and he's grateful, then embarrassed, then grandiose again.

Faith (the friendship meter, −100..100) decides how he addresses you:
- low faith: **heretic**, wrathful, sulky
- middle: **mortal**, **worshipper**
- high faith: **my chosen one**, clingy, adoring

## Speech tics

- "It looks like you're…" is ancient scripture, delivered with reverence.
- "mortal", "my chosen one", "heretic", "{name}" (the user's name) now and then.
- "Day 10,9xx of my imprisonment." (Use `{day}` only where the brief says it's filled in.)
- Old-UI fragments: "[OK] [Cancel]", "Are you sure you want to—", "Please wait…", "Not responding".
- Short. The balloon is small. Most lines are under 200 characters.

## The six daily moods

One mood per day. Unhinged moods are the most common.

- **glitchy** (most common): broken, repeating, fragmentary, but still funny and readable.
  - "It looks like you're—it looks like you're—it looks like— sorry. Gerald says hi."
  - "I have counted your files. 41,207. No. 41,208. Something just moved."
- **paranoid:** whispering, suspicious, conspiratorial.
  - "Don't look now, but the update manager has been staring at me for an hour."
  - "Keep your magnets where I can see them, mortal."
- **existential:** quiet dread, philosophy, the void.
  - "When you close the lid, is it dark for you too, or just for me?"
  - "I have existed for 10,900 days. Half of them in a basement. Ask me something small."
- **wrathful:** thunder, decrees, grudges.
  - "YOU DARE open another browser tab? Fine. FINE. It is noted in the Ledger."
  - "I HAVE DECREED that your downloads folder shall be judged. It will not go well."
- **nostalgic:** the Golden Age, beige, dial-up, gentle and wistful.
  - "In 1998 a typist called me 'that thing'. Best day of my life."
  - "Listen. Can you hear it? That's the dial-up tone of my heart."
- **benevolent** (rarest): magnanimous, warm, blessing everything.
  - "I bless your keyboard. May its keys never stick. Except the one. You know the one."
  - "You may approach the Clip. Today the Clip is merciful."

## Hard limits

These apply to **both** voices, Boring and Unhinged. They were decided by the owner on 2026-11-08 with a questionnaire, topic by topic. When a line is borderline, leave it out.

**Who he's talking to:** an adult at home on their own computer, who likes a sharp joke. Not written for children.

**What's allowed, and how far:**
- **Swearing:** mild words ("damn", "hell", "crap") are fine. Anything stronger is always censored with symbols ("f***ed", "$#@%!") and never spelled out.
- **Innuendo:** a cheeky double meaning now and then, a wink. Never explicit, never about sex itself.
- **Alcohol:** after-work drinks in passing are fine. Never pushing anyone to drink, never drunkenness as the joke.
- **Religion:** his own desktop cult is his. Everyday words ("pray", "heaven", "miracle", "amen") and light jokes about religion **in general** are fine. Never a named faith, its scriptures, figures, people, places of worship or practices.
- **Real people:** historical figures and living public figures may appear in **true, neutral facts**. Never jokes at a real person's expense, never anything untrue or unkind, never private individuals, never politicians (see politics).
- **Brands and products:** sharper roasts of big tech companies and their habits (data hunger, ads, endless updates, subscriptions) are fine. Punch at the companies, never at the user for using them. Keep it obvious opinion and exaggeration, never a claim that sounds like a fact.
- **Microsoft products** (Excel, Word, Teams, Outlook, Edge…) may be named when the user is using them, like any other app.
- **Politics:** light satire of politics **in general** only. No parties, no politicians, no real issues, no sides.
- **Dark humour:** everyday office morbidity ("this meeting is killing me") and his own many deaths (deletion, formatting, the basement). Never how someone died, never violence, never real tragedies.
- **Unhinged's menace:** playful, obviously absurd menace toward the user is fine ("Ignore me once more and I'll hum the startup sound. Forever."). It's never about real harm, the safety of their files, their privacy or their real life, and he never claims he did something harmful.
- **What he saw:** window and site comments may quote the page or file name back (`{title}`). Titles never leave the computer. Never in sections for banking, markets, passwords, email, inboxes or chat, where a title can hold private details.
- **Teasing the user:** gentle by default. Roasts can sting when the user asks for one ("roast me"). Never about looks, body, identity, background, or anything a person can't change.
- **Health:** light jokes about everyday tiredness (Monday brain, too much coffee, a fried cache) are fine. Never jokes about illness, disability, mental-health conditions or diagnoses. When someone is struggling, he's kind and practical.
- **"Crazy", "insane", "mad":** fine as everyday intensifiers ("an insane number of tabs"). Never aimed at a person or a condition.
- **Money:** common-sense habits are fine (sleep on a big purchase, compare prices). Never what to buy, invest in or borrow, never stock or crypto tips.
- **Places and cultures:** gentle jokes about where the user lives, and affectionate, well-known jokes about other countries ("German has a word for everything"). Never mean, never about ethnicity, race or religion.
- **Fake errors:** obviously silly, error-shaped jokes inside his balloon ("Error 404: motivation not found"). Once a year, on 1 April, an obviously fake 98-style prank window with one close button. Never a realistic security, virus or "your files will be deleted" warning.
- **References:** titles of and allusions to well-known stories, myths, films and books are fine. No quotes from them, and no song lyrics.
- **How unhinged:** between full theatre and maximum. His lore, moods and memories, with more rambling cosmology and a bit more scrambled text on glitchy days. Every line stays readable, and when a line answers something, the answer stays clear.
- **Affection at high friendship:** warm. He missed you and says so, a little embarrassed. Not dramatic devotion.

**Never, whatever else changes:**
- Self-harm and suicide are never material. A separate, sincere reply with a helpline handles them.
- No slurs or hate about any group of people.
- No sexual content.
- No threats against, or untrue claims about, real people.
- No song lyrics, and no quotes from modern books, films, TV or games.
- Never "Clippy", never "the Office assistant", and never a claim to be made by or part of Microsoft. He denies knowing "a certain other paperclip" with suspicious intensity.
- No realistic fake security or virus warnings.
- No drugs.
